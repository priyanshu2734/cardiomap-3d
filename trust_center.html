How to read this chart

Pick a risk threshold: the estimated risk at which someone would act (for example, refer for further testing). A low threshold means missing a case is considered much worse than a false alarm.

  1. Net benefit counts true positives and subtracts false positives, weighted by the odds pt/(1−pt) of the threshold. Higher is better.
  2. Treat all (dashed) acts on everyone; treat none (flat line at 0) acts on no one. The model is only useful where its line sits above both.
  3. The shaded band is a 95% bootstrap interval from resampling the records. If it overlaps treat-all or zero, the advantage is not established.
  4. Net true positives per 100 is net benefit × 100. Interventions avoided per 100 compares with treat-all: how many unnecessary actions the model saves per 100 patients without missing a true positive.

Out-of-fold, one referral dataset (303 records), so the band reflects sampling noise only, not a different population. Not a recommendation to treat.

Interpretation & limitations

  • Not clinical validation. These are cross-validation results on the supplied 303-record dataset, not an independent external clinical study.
  • Not individual certainty. Aggregate metrics do not tell you the probability that one person's prediction is correct.
  • Calibration is not fully characterized. The project stores Brier scores, but no calibration curve or calibration analysis is available here.
  • Vessel outputs need caution. The current evaluation summaries show lower F1 scores for LCX and RCA than CAD; do not interpret the 3D overlay as a measured lesion.
  • Generalization is unknown. Performance may differ across populations, settings, and data collection processes.
