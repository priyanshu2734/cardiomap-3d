Cardiac Research Lab · Pre-angiography triage

Who needs a closer look before the angiogram?

A chest-pain patient arrives. Using only non-invasive data, how well can we rank who likely has significant CAD (≥50% stenosis) and in which vessel (LAD, LCX or RCA), before an invasive angiogram? Five experiments test how much each kind of data and each model type adds, why LCX and RCA are harder, whether results hold across sex and age, and whether the probabilities can be trusted, with uncertainty shown for every number.

Experiment 1

Feature-group ablation

Which kinds of non-invasive data actually improve who we flag, and where does adding more stop mattering?

Adds data cumulatively (demographics & history, then labs, ECG, echo) for CAD, LAD, LCX and RCA, and gives each step's AUC change a paired 95% CI.

Run the experiment to see the AUC for each data tier with 95% bootstrap intervals, a plain-language triage takeaway, limitations and the full manifest.
Experiment 2

Model comparison

Does the choice of algorithm matter, or is the information in the data the limit?

Compares logistic regression, random forest and gradient boosting on every non-invasive feature; hyperparameters are chosen inside the inner CV.

Run the experiment to compare the three model families with paired 95% intervals on the AUC difference.
Experiment 3

Weak-vessel analysis (LCX and RCA)

Why are LCX and RCA harder to rank than LAD, and would more patients help?

Compares LCX and RCA with LAD (the reference): class balance, fold-to-fold variance, a learning curve on growing training sets, and plain-language reasons. Reasons are labelled as measured here or as general reasoning not tested here.

Run the experiment to see class balance, per-fold variance, a learning curve with 95% intervals and the reasons these vessels are harder.
Experiment 4

Subgroup check (sex and age band)

Do the numbers look similar across sex and age bands, and where is the group too small to say?

Descriptive only. Shows sample size, prevalence and out-of-fold performance for each group, with a warning when a group is small. No test between groups is run.

Run the experiment to see group sizes, prevalence and performance by sex and age band, with small-group warnings.
Experiment 5

Calibration and triage bands

When the tool gives a patient a high score, how many such patients truly had CAD?

Reliability curve, Brier score and calibration slope, then a triage view: for each probability band, how many patients truly had the condition.

Run the experiment to see the reliability curve, Brier score and how many patients in each probability band truly had CAD.
